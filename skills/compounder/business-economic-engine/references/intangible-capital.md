# Intangible Capital Diagnostic

Reported invested capital may omit economically important investment because accounting expenses items such as R&D, software development, customer acquisition, brand building, training, and organizational capability.

## Trigger

Run this diagnostic when a material part of growth depends on spending that creates multi-period benefits but is expensed immediately.

## Questions

1. Which operating expenses are plausibly investments with benefits beyond the current period?
2. Are they material enough to distort comparisons of margins, invested capital, reinvestment, or ROIC?
3. Is capitalization/adjustment feasible from disclosed data?
4. If not, what directional bias remains in reported economics?

## Treatment

- Use reported accounting metrics as facts.
- If an adjustment is defensible, label the adjusted figure `DERIVED` or `ESTIMATE`, disclose the capitalization/amortization assumption, and keep the reported figure visible.
- If adjustment is not defensible, do not fabricate precision. State the likely direction of distortion and reduce confidence.

## Common illusion

A software or branded consumer company can appear “asset-light” with extremely high reported ROIC because important reinvestment runs through the income statement. High reported ROIC is therefore not sufficient evidence of high incremental economic return until material intangible investment has been considered.
