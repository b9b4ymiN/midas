# Growth Architecture

Growth is an output. Decompose the causal engine before using historical CAGR as evidence of future compounding.

## Required bridge

Build the most evidence-backed version of:

`Capital / operating input → new economic units → volume / price / mix / capacity / geography / product / M&A → revenue → contribution profit → EBIT/NOPAT → FCF → per-share economics`

The exact drivers vary by business. Do not force all seven growth lanes when they are not economically relevant.

## Growth source decomposition

At minimum distinguish material contributions from:
- volume / customer or unit growth
- price
- mix
- new capacity / locations
- geography
- product / adjacency
- acquisitions

When precise percentages are unavailable, classify the contribution qualitatively and tag the conclusion `ESTIMATE`, `INFERENCE`, or `UNVERIFIED` rather than inventing a bridge.

## Capital-vs-output test

For each material growth lane ask:
1. What incremental capital or operating spend enables the growth?
2. What new operational output appears?
3. What revenue/gross profit/NOPAT/FCF follows after an economically sensible lag?
4. Does capital required per unit of output improve, hold, or deteriorate?
5. Does the result improve economics **per diluted share**?

Red flags include invested capital growing materially faster than normalized NOPAT, working capital absorbing increasing cash, CAC rising faster than customer contribution, capacity capex rising without matching economics, and M&A creating accounting EPS growth without acceptable post-deal returns.

## Scale economics and inflections

Classify scale direction when evidence permits:
- **STRENGTHENING** — density, utilization, procurement, operating leverage, or network effects improve marginal economics.
- **STABLE** — later units/cohorts retain broadly similar economics.
- **WEAKENING** — cannibalization, lower-quality geographies, rising CAC, complexity, or diseconomies reduce marginal economics.
- **UNRESOLVED** — evidence is insufficient.

Do not extrapolate early cohorts unchanged when later-vintage evidence exists.
