# Economic Unit Guide

The Economic Unit is the smallest repeatable unit that best reveals the economics of incremental growth. It is discovered from how the business scales, not chosen from an industry label.

## Discovery questions

1. What is added when the company grows: a customer, location, asset, transaction, contract, employee team, acquired business, or capacity block?
2. Which unit has a measurable acquisition/build cost and cash contribution?
3. Which unit reveals maturity, retention, utilization, density, or payback?
4. Which unit links most cleanly to corporate revenue and invested capital?

## Typical examples

| Business pattern | Candidate unit | Useful diagnostics |
|---|---|---|
| Retail/restaurant | Store/cohort | sales/store, capex/store, mature margin, payback, cannibalization |
| SaaS/subscription | Customer/cohort | CAC, retention, NRR, gross margin, payback, contribution margin |
| Hospital | Bed/facility/patient episode | utilization, revenue/bed, facility ramp, capex, mature ROIC |
| Data center | MW/campus | contracted MW, build cost/MW, utilization, yield, power constraints |
| Marketplace | Buyer/seller/transaction cohort | frequency, retention, take rate, contribution margin, density |
| Industrial | Production line/capacity block | capex/unit capacity, utilization, asset turns, incremental margin |
| Consumer hardware / product-cycle | Device shipment + product generation | units, ASP, gross margin, R&D/launch spend, channel inventory, cross-generation repeatability |
| Serial acquirer | Acquisition cohort | purchase multiple, post-acquisition cash return, organic growth, reinvestment |

## Micro → Corporate reconciliation

Good unit economics are not sufficient. Reconcile unit results against corporate economics:

`Units × revenue/unit → revenue → gross/contribution profit → central costs → operating profit → cash/NOPAT → invested capital`

Investigate unexplained leakage from overhead, stock compensation, working capital, maintenance capex, churn, or central infrastructure.

## Multiple engines

Use multiple units when different segments have materially different capital, margin, duration, or customer behavior. Consolidate only after each engine is understood.


## Product-cycle caution

A shipment or launch can look like an Economic Unit without being a durable reinvestment unit. For hardware, games, media, fashion, drugs, and other generation-dependent businesses, test whether economics repeat **across product cycles** after R&D/launch spend, cannibalization, inventory/returns, channel incentives, and competitive response.

Classify the engine as:
- **REPEATABLE** — multiple generations demonstrate comparable or improving owner economics.
- **CYCLE_DEPENDENT** — the next generation must recreate demand/economics and persistence is not yet demonstrated.
- **UNRESOLVED** — insufficient cross-generation evidence.

One successful launch is operating evidence; it is not Level-5 persistence.
