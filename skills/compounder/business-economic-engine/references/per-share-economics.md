# Per-Share Economics

The owner ultimately holds a fraction of the business, not the aggregate company. Material aggregate growth must therefore be translated into economics per diluted share.

## Required diagnostics

Track when available:
- basic and diluted weighted-average shares
- period-end diluted-equivalent share count where useful
- stock-based compensation and option/RSU issuance
- shares issued for acquisitions or financing
- repurchases and net change after issuance
- revenue / diluted share
- NOPAT / diluted share
- FCF / diluted share
- owner-earnings proxy / diluted share when defined explicitly

Use consistent share definitions across periods. Explain material changes from splits, conversions, acquisitions, treasury stock, or option dilution.

## Interpretation

Aggregate growth is not equivalent to owner compounding.

Example: net income grows 5× while diluted shares grow 4×. The company is much larger, but earnings per diluted share rise only 25%.

Treat buybacks economically, not narratively: a repurchase program is not positive evidence if SBC/acquisition issuance offsets it or if capital was deployed at poor prices. Price discipline belongs to allocator analysis; the Business Engine only establishes the net ownership effect.

## Output rule

For every material growth conclusion state whether per-share economics are:
- **IMPROVING**
- **FLAT**
- **DETERIORATING**
- **UNRESOLVED**

If aggregate growth and per-share growth diverge materially, surface the divergence as an economic leakage or unresolved allocator issue for downstream analysis.
