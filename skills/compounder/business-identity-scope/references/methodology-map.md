# Business Identity & Market Scope — Methodology Map

Purpose: route a framing question to the smallest proven framework that can answer it. Frameworks are tools, not report sections; do not run all of them mechanically.

| Research question | Primary lens | Trigger | Required evidence | Output | Main failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Is the company being defined too narrowly by its current product? | Levitt — Marketing Myopia | Sector/product label dominates the framing | customer need, substitutes, management description, actual buyer behavior | broader candidate business definition | expanding so broadly that the definition loses economic meaning |
| Which customers, needs, and delivery technologies define the actual arena? | Abell — Who × What × How | multiple customer groups/functions/technologies make boundaries ambiguous | customer groups, customer functions/jobs, solution technology/route | explicit business-definition cube | confusing product features with customer functions |
| What is the customer really hiring the product/service to do? | Christensen et al. — Jobs to Be Done | substitutes cross conventional category lines or purchase occasion matters | occasion, alternatives considered, switching behavior, repeat use | customer-job and substitute arena | inferring a job from demographics or marketing copy alone |
| Which capabilities can open adjacent markets? | Prahalad & Hamel — Core Competence | management claims adjacency based on a transferable capability | demonstrated skill, access to multiple markets, customer benefit, difficulty to imitate | capability-transfer map | treating any technical ability as a core competence |
| Is an adjacency core, emerging, or merely optional? | McKinsey — Three Horizons | current business, scaling venture, and experiments coexist | revenue/profit, deployments, customers, committed capital, pilots/R&D | evidence-maturity arena map | treating Horizon 3 as base-case market scope |
| How should the potential market be defined before estimating future revenue? | Damodaran — Young Growth Value Drivers | market size changes materially under narrow vs broad product definitions | product/service definition, market evolution, competitive set, resources required for share | candidate potential-market boundary | choosing a wide definition only because it produces a larger TAM |
| Is a historical analogue structurally useful? | Gavetti & Rivkin — Analogical Reasoning | analyst wants to transfer lessons from Starbucks/Amazon/Tesla/etc. | customer job, economic unit, route-to-market, capital model, power source, growth mechanism | transferable vs non-transferable lessons | surface analogy / sector similarity |

## Routing rule

1. Start from evidence about current economics, not management aspiration.
2. Invoke a lens only when its trigger is present.
3. Preserve multiple frames when evidence conflicts; do not force one prematurely.
4. Classify arenas by evidence maturity before any downstream TAM work.
5. Record disconfirming evidence that could invalidate the candidate frame.

## STOP boundary

This methodology does **not** estimate TAM, category growth, competitive share, valuation, or compounder quality. It frames the arena so downstream research asks the right questions.
