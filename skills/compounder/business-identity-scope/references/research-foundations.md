# Research Foundations — Business Identity & Market Scope

These sources justify Layer 0 methodology. They are not authorities that override company-specific evidence.

## Theodore Levitt — Marketing Myopia
- Author: Theodore Levitt
- Title: *Marketing Myopia*
- Original idea: 1960; HBR reprint/page currently available as 2004 edition
- URL: https://hbr.org/2004/07/marketing-myopia
- Concept used: define a business around customer need rather than current product.
- Layer 0 role: test whether a product/sector label is too narrow.
- Limitation: broad customer-need definitions can become economically meaningless unless bounded by evidence.

## Derek F. Abell — Defining the Business
- Author: Derek F. Abell
- Title: *Defining the Business: The Starting Point of Strategic Planning*
- Year: 1980
- URL: https://books.google.com/books/about/Defining_the_Business.html?id=nJMoAQAAMAAJ
- Concept used: business boundaries across customer groups, customer functions, and alternative technologies/means.
- Layer 0 role: structure ambiguous business definitions.
- Limitation: it is a strategic segmentation framework, not a market-size or investment-return model.

## Christensen, Hall, Dillon & Duncan — Jobs to Be Done
- Authors: Clayton M. Christensen, Taddy Hall, Karen Dillon, David S. Duncan
- Title: *Know Your Customers’ “Jobs to Be Done”*
- Year: 2016
- URL: https://hbr.org/2016/09/know-your-customers-jobs-to-be-done
- Concept used: infer competition from the job/occasion that causes customer choice.
- Layer 0 role: discover demand-side substitutes that sector labels miss.
- Limitation: jobs require behavioral evidence; demographic or branding stories are insufficient.

## C.K. Prahalad & Gary Hamel — Core Competence
- Authors: C.K. Prahalad, Gary Hamel
- Title: *The Core Competence of the Corporation*
- Year: 1990
- URL: https://hbr.org/1990/05/the-core-competence-of-the-corporation
- Concept used: a core competence should provide access to multiple markets, contribute materially to customer benefit, and be difficult to imitate.
- Layer 0 role: distinguish transferable capability from product adjacency fantasy.
- Limitation: competence alone does not prove customers, profit pools, or attractive economics in an adjacent market.

## Baghai, Coley & White — Three Horizons
- Authors/book lineage: Mehrdad Baghai, Stephen Coley, David White; *The Alchemy of Growth* (1999)
- McKinsey summary: *Enduring Ideas: The three horizons of growth*
- Date: 2009 summary
- URL: https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/strategy-and-corporate-finance/our-insights/enduring-ideas-the-three-horizons-of-growth
- Concept used: distinguish core cash/profit businesses, emerging growth businesses, and future options/pilots.
- Layer 0 role: evidence-stage market arenas without double counting optionality.
- Limitation: horizons describe maturity/portfolio role; they do not prove attractiveness or returns.

## Aswath Damodaran — Young Growth Value Drivers
- Author: Aswath Damodaran
- Title: *Young growth companies — Value Drivers*
- URL: https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/littlebook/younggrowthvaluedrivers.htm
- Concept used: future revenue depends first on defining the product/service and potential market, then market evolution and attainable share.
- Layer 0 role: make market definition an explicit assumption before TAM/share estimation.
- Limitation: the framework still requires an analyst to justify how broad the definition should be; Layer 0 adds evidence staging and stress tests for that problem.

## Giovanni Gavetti & Jan W. Rivkin — Analogical Reasoning
- Authors: Giovanni Gavetti, Jan W. Rivkin
- Title: *How Strategists Really Think: Tapping the Power of Analogy*
- Year: 2005
- URL: https://hbr.org/2005/04/how-strategists-really-think-tapping-the-power-of-analogy
- Concept used: analogies can aid strategic reasoning but surface similarity creates systematic errors.
- Layer 0 role: require structural matching and transfer limits before using a business analogue.
- Limitation: an analogue supplies hypotheses and reference classes, not proof that the focal company will follow the same path.
