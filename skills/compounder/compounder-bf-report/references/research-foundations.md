# Research Foundations and Methodology Provenance

This package synthesizes original ideas and empirical evidence; it does not replace the underlying sources. For live investment work, consult the current original document where possible and record the exact source used in the Evidence Ledger.

## Provenance fields

Each foundation below records:
- **Publication date** — date of the cited source/version when known.
- **Concept used** — the analytical idea inherited by the pipeline.
- **Source role** — North Star / measurement / empirical challenge / checklist / governance prior.
- **Limitation** — what the source does *not* prove and how the skill must avoid overreach.

## Christopher W. Mayer

### *100 Baggers: Stocks That Return 100-to-One and How to Find Them*
- **Author:** Christopher W. Mayer
- **Publication date:** 2015
- **URL:** https://www.harriman-house.com/100baggers
- **Concept used:** Long-duration compounding; growth + quality + patience; 100-bagger as a research destination rather than a mechanical screen.
- **Source role:** **North Star**.
- **Limitation:** Retrospective winner study; does not provide a complete predictive formula for future 100-baggers.

### IIC 2021: A Conversation with Chris Mayer – Author of 100 Baggers
- **Speaker:** Christopher W. Mayer; CFA Society India summary of interview with Sanjay Bakshi
- **Publication date:** 21 May 2021
- **URL:** https://cfasocietyindia.org/blog/iic-2021-a-conversation-with-chris-mayer-author-of-100-baggers/
- **Concept used:** Mayer's post-book emphasis on high return on capital, repeated reinvestment, sustainability of the spread, allocator quality, owner-operator as a non-mechanical prior, and the importance of stability/debt.
- **Source role:** **North Star update / practitioner clarification**.
- **Limitation:** Interview summary, not a controlled empirical study; statements are investment judgment, not universal causal laws.

## Chuck Akre / Akre Capital

### “What Do We Mean By Reinvestment?”
- **Author:** Chuck Akre
- **Publication date:** 20 October 2014
- **URL:** https://www.akrecapital.com/what-do-we-mean-by-reinvestment/
- **Concept used:** Compounding depends on an extraordinary business, people, and abundant reinvestment opportunity; long-run business-value compounding is shaped by returns on reinvested capital.
- **Source role:** **Reinvestment architecture / practitioner framework**.
- **Limitation:** Conceptual practitioner framework; does not prescribe one accounting formula for marginal return across all business models.

## Aswath Damodaran / NYU Stern

### “The Fundamental Determinants of Growth”
- **Author:** Aswath Damodaran
- **Publication date:** Living NYU Stern teaching resource; accessed 2026-08-19
- **URL:** https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/valquestions/growth.htm
- **Concept used:** Growth from new investments is driven by reinvestment and returns on **future/marginal** investments; average and marginal returns can differ materially.
- **Source role:** **Measurement / growth economics**.
- **Limitation:** Framework requires economically sensible accounting adjustments and lag assumptions; simple ΔNOPAT/Δcapital can be noisy.

### “Young Growth Companies – Value Drivers”
- **Author:** Aswath Damodaran
- **Publication date:** Living NYU Stern teaching resource; accessed 2026-08-19
- **URL:** https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/littlebook/younggrowthvaluedrivers.htm
- **Concept used:** Young-company analysis through revenue opportunity, mature margin, capital required for growth, and implied future economics rather than requiring long reported ROIC history.
- **Source role:** **Emerging-compounder measurement**.
- **Limitation:** Mature margins and capital efficiency may be assumptions when disclosure is limited; the skill must label them explicitly rather than convert them to facts.

## Michael Mauboussin & Dan Callahan / Morgan Stanley Counterpoint Global

### “Return on Invested Capital: How to Calculate ROIC and Handle Common Issues”
- **Authors:** Michael Mauboussin, Dan Callahan
- **Publication date:** 6 October 2022
- **URL:** https://www.morganstanley.com/im/en-sg/institutional-investor/insights/articles/return-on-invested-capital.html
- **Concept used:** ROIC measurement, links to FCF/economic profit/growth, practical calculation issues, empirical distributions, and intangible-investment adjustments.
- **Source role:** **ROIC measurement / accounting challenge**.
- **Limitation:** Reported/adjusted ROIC remains an average metric; Future Compounder analysis must still investigate marginal/cohort economics.

### “ROIC and the Investment Process: ROICs, How They Change, and Shareholder Returns”
- **Authors:** Michael Mauboussin, Dan Callahan
- **Publication date:** 6 June 2023
- **URL:** https://www.morganstanley.com/im/en-us/institutional-investor/insights/consilient-observer/roic-and-the-investment-process.html
- **Concept used:** ROIC transitions, mean reversion, sector differences, and interpretation of changes in return quality.
- **Source role:** **Empirical return-persistence context**.
- **Limitation:** Association between ROIC changes and shareholder returns does not by itself identify causal future compounders.

### “Capital Allocation: Results, Analysis, and Assessment”
- **Authors:** Michael Mauboussin, Dan Callahan
- **Publication date:** 5 November 2025 update
- **URL:** https://www.morganstanley.com/im/en-gb/intermediary-investor/insights/consilient-observer/capital-allocation.html
- **Concept used:** Sources/uses of capital, intangible investment, historical allocation patterns, ROIC, incentives, and assessment of allocator skill.
- **Source role:** **Capital-allocation measurement / empirical context**.
- **Limitation:** Historical allocation outcomes require company-specific attribution and cannot be reduced to a single universal hurdle-rate score.

### “Bayes and Base Rates: How History Can Guide Our Assessment of the Future”
- **Authors:** Michael Mauboussin, Dan Callahan
- **Publication date:** 10 February 2026
- **URL:** https://www.morganstanley.com/im/en-us/institutional-investor/insights/consilient-observer/bayes-and-base-rates.html
- **Concept used:** Start with a reference-class prior, then update with company-specific evidence; base rates discipline optimistic long-range forecasts.
- **Source role:** **Outside view / Bayesian discipline**.
- **Limitation:** Reference-class selection is judgmental and historical base rates do not make exceptional outcomes impossible.

### “Competitive Advantage Period: The Neglected Value Driver”
- **Authors:** Michael Mauboussin, Dan Callahan
- **Publication date:** 14 April 2026
- **URL:** https://www.morganstanley.com/im/en-us/institutional-investor/insights/consilient-observer/the-neglected-value-driver.html
- **Concept used:** Anticipated value creation depends on ROIC spread, amount invested, and how long the positive spread can persist; CAP formalizes Mayer's longevity question.
- **Source role:** **Duration / competitive-advantage framework**.
- **Limitation:** Market-implied CAP is valuation-oriented; Core Future Compounder uses the duration/mean-reversion insight but does not perform valuation.

## Empirical challenge layer

### Chan, Karceski & Lakonishok — “The Level and Persistence of Growth Rates”
- **Authors:** Louis K.C. Chan, Jason Karceski, Josef Lakonishok
- **Publication date:** NBER Working Paper May 2001; Journal of Finance publication April 2003
- **URL:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w8282
- **Concept used:** Very high long-term growth exists but is relatively rare; long-term growth has limited persistence/predictability; analyst long-term forecasts tend to be optimistic.
- **Source role:** **Empirical challenge / base rate**.
- **Limitation:** Historical sample and metrics do not prove that no modern company can sustain exceptional growth; use as a prior, not a veto.

### Benjamin Maury — “Sustainable competitive advantage and profitability persistence: Sources versus outcomes for assessing advantage”
- **Publication date:** Journal of Business Research, March 2018
- **URL:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0148296317304344
- **Concept used:** Observable outcomes such as sustained profitability and sustained market share can be more informative for future profitability persistence than some traditional barrier-to-entry measures.
- **Source role:** **Moat-outcome empirical challenge**.
- **Limitation:** Cross-sectional international evidence does not identify the correct economic unit for each company and should not turn moat outcomes into a mechanical screen.

### Rüdiger Fahlenbrach — “Founder-CEOs, Investment Decisions, and Stock Market Performance”
- **Publication date:** Journal of Financial and Quantitative Analysis, April 2009 (working-paper versions earlier)
- **URL:** https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/abs/founderceos-investment-decisions-and-stock-market-performance/99BFE8A8C1A42F8CC7E32430FD627783
- **Concept used:** Founder-CEO firms in the studied sample differed in investment behavior and performance; supports treating ownership/alignment as a potentially informative prior.
- **Source role:** **Governance prior / empirical context**.
- **Limitation:** Historical U.S. sample; founder status is not proof of governance quality, future returns, or minority-shareholder alignment.

## Usage rule

**Mayer sets the destination. Akre clarifies the reinvestment machine. Damodaran improves measurement, especially marginal and young-company economics. Mauboussin/Callahan add ROIC, allocation, base-rate, and duration discipline. Motilal provides research-structure precedent. Empirical studies are used to challenge favored narratives.**

If a foundation conflicts with company-specific primary evidence or newer robust empirical work, record the contradiction rather than forcing the evidence to fit the framework.
