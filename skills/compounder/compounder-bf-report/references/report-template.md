# Future Compounder BF Report Template v2.2

Use only decision-relevant sections, but preserve the causal order: **Scope → Market & Growth → Economics → Reinvestment → Falsification**. Do not turn frameworks into a checklist dump.

## 1. Executive Summary
5–10 sentences: business frame, external growth system, economic engine, why compounding may persist, strongest evidence, strongest contradiction, Potential / Evidence Maturity / Confidence, and the most important unresolved item.

## 2. Business Identity & Market Scope
State the Layer 0 candidate definition, Proven/Emerging/Option arenas, main alternative frame, structural analogues with transfer limits, and any `SCOPE_CHALLENGE`.

## 3. Market & Growth System
Explain the external opportunity before internal returns:
- category/demand regime and causal demand drivers;
- major structural/cyclical tailwinds/headwinds;
- metric-comparability breaks;
- substitutes/customer jobs where material.

## 4. Industry Structure, Profit Pool & Value Migration
Where material, show who captures profit across the value chain, bargaining-power direction, structural competition, and whether value is migrating toward or away from the company’s business design.

## 5. Competitive Position & Share Mechanism
Show direct competitors/substitutes, market-share direction, relative growth, and **why** share should change. Distinguish evidenced share gain from share purchased through price, capital, acquisition, or deteriorating economics.

## 6. Growth Decomposition
Separate strategic attribution—portfolio/category/geography momentum, market-share change, M&A/divestiture—from operating mechanisms such as volume, price, mix, existing-unit productivity, new units, product, customer usage, channel, and geography. Avoid double counting.

## 7. Customer, Channel & International Growth
For material channels, explain net incrementality, cannibalization, halo/recapture, penetration/frequency/availability as relevant. For international growth, show local replication evidence, CAGE/AAA implications where useful, and country/cohort differences. Store/channel count alone is not success.

## 8. Growth Execution & Evidence Trajectory
Track important initiatives as Promise → Action → Result. State whether external-growth evidence is improving, stable, deteriorating, or unresolved.

## 9. Business & Economic Engine
Business model, economic units, unit/cohort economics, repeatability/product-cycle dependency, and operating flow from input/capital to cash.

## 10. Growth Architecture → Economics
Start from Layer 1 growth drivers and show the bridge from driver → investment/input → operational output → revenue → contribution/NOPAT/FCF. Separate external opportunity from what that growth actually earns.

## 11. Micro → Corporate → Per-Share Economics
Reconcile unit economics to consolidated EBIT/NOPAT/FCF and **per-share** outcomes. Show diluted share-count/SBC/issuance leakage where material.

## 12. Return Economics
Historical returns vs incremental/marginal returns; scale direction; accounting/intangible-capital distortion; direction of change and maturity lag.

## 13. Reinvestment Machine & Capital Allocation
Where cash/capital goes; reinvestment capacity; acquisition-cohort economics; net buyback/dilution effects; allocator behavior and governance where material.

## 14. Runway & Financial Resilience
Reconcile Layer 1 external-growth runway with economic capacity, realistic share, unit returns, saturation/cannibalization, working-capital/funding needs, leverage/refinancing, and whether market runway is financeable.

## 15. Duration / Moat Outcomes
Pricing power, retention, share/profitability persistence, scale/density, competitive response, and reasons excess returns may mean-revert.

## 16. Emerging Compounder Analysis
Only when applicable: unit/cohort evidence, mature-economics bridge, external evidence trajectory, and why short history changes Evidence Maturity rather than automatically changing Potential.

## 17. Outside View / Base Rate
State the relevant prior/reference class, company-specific evidence that updates it, and residual uncertainty. Do not use base rates as a hard reject.

## 18. Evidence Ladder
Summarize material evidence from Story → Operating/Market → Unit Economics → Corporate/Per-share Translation → Persistence, then explain the Evidence Maturity label.

## 19. Counter-Thesis / Grill
Strongest opposing interpretation, including category-tailwind illusion, profit-pool/value-migration risk, share-gain quality, channel cannibalization, international replication failure, marginal-return decay, scale failure, allocator failure, accounting distortion, governance concerns, and critical unknowns.

## 20. Reverse Reality Check
Show business requirements for a 10x case and optional 100x stress case: buyers/units/capacity, category size/share, revenue, margin, capital, funding, and dilution. This is a plausibility check, **not valuation or target price**.

## 21. Potential / Evidence Maturity / Confidence
Report the three dimensions separately and justify each.

## 22. Kill Conditions
Observable developments that invalidate or materially weaken the thesis.

## 23. Data Gaps & Next Evidence
Decision-relevant missing data and the source/event that could resolve each gap.

## 24. Sources, Evidence & Methodology Notes
Trace major claims to the shared Evidence Ledger and original sources. Identify methodology foundations only when they materially shape interpretation.

## 25. Disclaimer
Research and educational output only. Not financial advice. Conditional plans and open research questions only - never an instruction to buy, sell, or hold.
