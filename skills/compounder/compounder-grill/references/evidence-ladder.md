# Evidence Ladder

Evidence Maturity is based on **what has been demonstrated**, not the age of the company alone.

## Five levels

### Level 1 — Story
Management narrative, TAM claims, strategic intent, claimed moat. Useful for hypotheses, not proof.

### Level 2 — Operating Indicators
Customer/store/unit growth, retention signals, utilization, order/frequency, market-share movement, pricing behavior.

### Level 3 — Unit Economics
Observed cohort/unit return, payback, contribution economics, capital required, retention/expansion economics, mature-unit performance.

### Level 4 — Corporate Translation
Unit economics reconcile to consolidated EBIT/NOPAT/FCF and **per-share** owner economics after central costs, working capital, SBC/dilution, and maintenance investment.

### Level 5 — Persistence
Economics survive multiple cohorts, scale steps, geographies, competitive responses, or cycles without material marginal-return decay.

## Mapping to Evidence Maturity

The mapping is judgmental, not mechanical:
- **Early** — mostly Levels 1–2; Level 3 may be partial.
- **Developing** — credible Level 3 and some Level 4 evidence.
- **Established** — repeated Level 3–4 evidence with partial Level 5.
- **Deep** — strong Level 5 evidence across meaningful stresses/cycles.

A young company can have Exceptional Potential with Early/Developing Evidence. A mature company can have Deep Evidence of mediocre or decaying economics.

## Confidence is separate

Confidence also depends on source quality, measurement reliability, contradictions, assumption sensitivity, and critical unknowns. Never convert Evidence Ladder level directly into Confidence or Potential.
