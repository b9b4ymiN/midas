# Reverse 10x / 100x Business Reality Check

This is **not valuation** and does not produce a target price. It stress-tests whether a large compounding narrative requires a physically or economically impossible future business.

## Principle

Start with a 10x case because it is more decision-useful. Use 100x only as an optional extreme stress case when relevant to the user's question.

## Declare the horizon first

The check is meaningless without one, and an undeclared horizon is how a demanding test quietly becomes an impossible one. State the horizon, and **tie it to the Duration evidence** — the period over which the moat and the return spread have been argued to hold. Do not adopt ten years by habit.

Note the arithmetic the horizon controls: 10x over ten years requires about 26% per year; over twenty years, about 12%. The same "10x" is two different claims.

## Anchor on the operating business, not the market price

**The primary anchor is always operational** — revenue, units, customers, capacity, or share. Reverse-engineer, as transparently as evidence permits:

- required economic units / customers / capacity
- required revenue
- required normalized margin / NOPAT / FCF economics
- required market share / penetration
- capital required to build or acquire that scale
- financing dependence and required dilution
- reinvestment return and duration implied by the path

A market-capitalization anchor is **optional and secondary**. It may be used, but going from market value to required earnings requires assuming a constant multiple, which is a valuation judgement — precisely what this check claims not to make. If used, label the market capitalization as a current observed market fact, state the multiple assumption on the same line, and never present the result without the operational path beside it.

Where both are shown and they disagree, the operational path governs.

## Report a required rate, not a verdict word

The output is a number that can be compared, not a label that ends the conversation:

```
Required compounding rate = (target multiple)^(1 / horizon years) − 1

Compare against three references:
  1. what the company has actually achieved       — its own historical per-share rate
  2. what the engine can fund                     — durable growth from the reinvestment layer
  3. the base rate for the reference class        — for 100x, Mayer's sample needed
                                                    roughly 20–26% per year for 17–25 years
```

Reporting "IMPLAUSIBLE" alone tells the reader nothing about *how far away* the path is. A company needing 26% while achieving 13% and funding 10% is a specific, useful finding: the multiple is out of reach while the business is performing well. That distinction is the point of the exercise.

State separately what **is** supported. If 10x is out of reach but 3–4x over the same horizon follows from the durable-growth figure, say so — a compounding thesis does not require a 10x to be worth holding, and pretending otherwise pushes the analysis toward narratives that do.

## Reality tests

Flag the path when it requires, for example:
- >100% market share or implausible serviceable-market capture
- a revenue figure exceeding the credible size of the whole industry
- unit density/capacity beyond physical or regulatory constraints
- mature margins structurally above credible economics without a mechanism
- capital needs inconsistent with funding capacity
- dilution that destroys per-share compounding
- no plausible way to preserve incremental returns at the required scale

## Output states

The label summarizes the comparison; it does not replace it.

- **PLAUSIBLE** — required rate is within reach of both the achieved rate and what the engine can fund.
- **STRETCHED** — required rate exceeds both, but by a margin execution could close.
- **IMPLAUSIBLE** — a core requirement violates a credible market or economic constraint, regardless of execution.
- **UNRESOLVED** — insufficient evidence to perform a meaningful check.

Keep assumptions explicit and tagged. Do not use this check to claim a stock “will” 10x/100x.
