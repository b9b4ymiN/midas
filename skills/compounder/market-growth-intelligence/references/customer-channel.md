# Customer & Channel Incrementality

## Trigger
Material new channel, route-to-market, product line, digital ordering path, marketplace, delivery platform, distributor, store-format change, or footprint/capacity expansion with cannibalization risk.

## Incrementality test
Ask whether growth comes from:
- new buyers/customers;
- new usage occasions;
- higher frequency/usage;
- broader physical/mental availability;
- recapture/halo across channels;
minus:
- cannibalization of existing products/channels;
- migration caused by discounts or marketing reallocation.

## Status
`HIGH`, `MODERATE`, `LOW`, `NEGATIVE`, `UNRESOLVED`.

## Expansion Incrementality
For new stores/facilities/capacity, test whether footprint growth adds system demand or redistributes it from existing units. Use same-store/same-unit productivity, local density, recapture/halo, and system-level growth. A rising store count alongside falling existing-unit productivity is a cannibalization warning, not proof of failure by itself.

## Sell-in vs sell-through
If revenue/units reflect shipments to distributors or retailers, inspect channel inventory, returns, activation/usage, end registrations, or other sell-through evidence before calling the growth end demand.

## Channel economics evidence
Where disclosed, note commission/take rate, fulfillment cost, customer-data ownership, bargaining power, capacity utilization, and contribution direction. Full return analysis remains downstream.

## Consumer brand routing
When relevant, examine penetration/buyer growth, frequency, availability, and Category Entry Points rather than relying on “loyal fans” rhetoric.

## Failure mode
“Delivery GMV +80%” is not +80% incremental company demand unless new buyers/occasions and cannibalization are addressed.
