# Demand & Category Evolution

## Trigger
Always.

## Start with the demand unit
Use the business archetype rather than a generic TAM formula.

- Consumer: buyers/penetration × frequency/occasions × spend/mix.
- Subscription/B2B: customers × seats/usage × retention/expansion × price.
- Platform: active sides × transaction frequency × volume/GMV.
- Industrial/capacity: installed base/projects/capacity × utilization × price/mix.

## Questions
1. What has actually driven category volume/value historically, and is the evidence sell-in or end-customer sell-through/usage?
2. Is growth from penetration, frequency, price/mix, geography, regulation, technology, or substitution?
3. Which drivers are structural versus cyclical?
4. Is the category creating new occasions/jobs or merely shifting spend inside the same occasion?
5. What evidence would show saturation or reversal?

## Category regime
`STRUCTURAL_TAILWIND`, `CYCLICAL_TAILWIND`, `STABLE`, `CYCLICAL_HEADWIND`, `STRUCTURAL_HEADWIND`, `UNRESOLVED`.

## Evidence discipline
Consultant forecasts are estimates/market expectations. Prefer observed volume, penetration, usage, price, cohort, regulator/government, and competitor evidence.

## Conditional consumer routing
Use Category Entry Points / Mental and Physical Availability when the company is a consumer brand and buying occasions/availability are material. Do not force these metrics onto industrial or B2B cases.


## Sell-in / sell-through guard
For hardware, semiconductors, pharmaceuticals, distributors, dealer networks, and similar models, shipments into the channel may lead or lag end demand. Check distributor/retailer inventory, returns, utilization/activation, end-market registrations, or other sell-through proxies before calling shipment growth a category tailwind.
