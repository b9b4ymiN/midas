# Growth Decomposition

## Trigger
Always when discussing company growth.

## Level A — Strategic attribution
Start with:
- `PORTFOLIO_MOMENTUM` — market growth in the categories/geographies where the company already participates;
- `SHARE_CHANGE` — organic gain/loss relative to those markets;
- `M&A_DIVESTITURE` — purchased/sold revenue or activity.

Do not force precise percentages when public data do not permit them. Directional attribution with `UNRESOLVED` residuals is preferable to fake precision.

## Level B — Operating-driver bridge
Explain how the attribution manifests operationally:
- volume/customers/transactions/capacity;
- price;
- mix;
- existing-unit/same-store/same-customer productivity;
- new units/locations/capacity **net of cannibalization/density effects**;
- products;
- channels;
- geography;
- retention/frequency/usage.

## Reconciliation rule
A driver belongs once. For example, a new delivery channel that mostly captures existing store customers is not separately additive to category/share/store growth. M&A-created units are not organic new-unit growth.

## Comparability
Run the Metric Comparability Gate before using historical growth series.

## Handoff
Layer 1 establishes growth source. Business Economic Engine translates those drivers into revenue→profit/NOPAT→FCF→per-share economics and capital/output.


## Expansion Incrementality
For stores, facilities, capacity, routes, sales territories, or other footprint growth, compare system growth with existing-unit productivity. New-unit sales are not fully incremental if nearby/existing units lose volume. Where data permit, bridge: new-unit contribution − existing-unit cannibalization ± halo/recapture. Otherwise classify expansion incrementality as `UNRESOLVED`.
