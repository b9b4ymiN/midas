# Market & Growth Intelligence — Research Foundations

This file records the external research foundations used by Layer 1. Framework names are not mandatory report headings; each source is used only for the question it resolves.

## 1. McKinsey — The Granularity of Growth
- Organization/authors: McKinsey & Company; Mehrdad Baghai, Sven Smit, Patrick Viguerie and related McKinsey research
- Publication: 2007 book / McKinsey articles from 2005 onward
- URL: https://www.mckinsey.com/featured-insights/employment-and-growth/the-granularity-of-growth
- Concept used: Decompose corporate growth into portfolio momentum, M&A/divestitures, and market-share performance; analyze at granular segment/category/geography level.
- Layer 1 role: Backbone for strategic growth attribution before drilling into operating drivers.
- Limitation: Original empirical work focused heavily on larger public companies. Smaller/emerging companies can derive more growth from share gains, so the decomposition is a starting attribution framework, not a universal weighting rule.

## 2. McKinsey — Drawing a New Road Map for Growth
- Organization/authors: McKinsey & Company
- Publication date: April 1, 2011
- URL: https://www.mckinsey.com/featured-insights/employment-and-growth/drawing-a-new-road-map-for-growth
- Concept used: Smaller companies can exhibit different growth patterns, with share gain contributing more than for large incumbents.
- Layer 1 role: Prevent misuse of large-company base rates when analyzing emerging compounders.
- Limitation: Cross-company averages do not establish the causal mechanism for any one company's share gain.

## 3. Michael E. Porter — Five Forces / Industry Structure
- Organization/author: Harvard Business School, Michael E. Porter
- Original concept: 1979; official HBS reference page current
- URL: https://www.isc.hbs.edu/strategy/business-strategy/Pages/the-five-forces.aspx
- Concept used: Rivalry, entrants, substitutes, supplier power, and buyer power determine how economic value is divided and how industry profitability changes.
- Layer 1 role: Diagnose value capture and structural change, not merely list competitors.
- Limitation: Industry-structure analysis alone does not explain why one company will outperform within the same industry; company-specific power and execution still require evidence.

## 4. Orit Gadiesh & James L. Gilbert — Profit Pools
- Publication date: May–June 1998
- URL: https://hbr.org/1998/05/how-to-map-your-industrys-profit-pool
- Concept used: Revenue pools and profit pools can differ sharply across a value chain; profitable growth requires knowing where profits are earned and how that distribution changes.
- Layer 1 role: Prevent “large/fast-growing market = attractive company economics” reasoning.
- Limitation: Profit-pool mapping can be data intensive and often relies on estimates for private actors; uncertainty must remain explicit.

## 5. Adrian J. Slywotzky — Value Migration
- Publication: 1996, Harvard Business School Press
- URL: https://books.google.com/books/about/Value_Migration.html?id=7bPJwAEACAAJ
- Concept used: Economic value can migrate from old business designs to designs that better satisfy changing customer priorities and capture profits differently.
- Layer 1 role: Identify structural shifts between business models, activities, or layers rather than assuming category leaders keep the profit pool.
- Limitation: Primarily a strategic framework supported by historical cases; use as a hypothesis generator and seek direct operating evidence before declaring value migration.

## 6. Clayton Christensen, Taddy Hall, Karen Dillon & David Duncan — Jobs to Be Done
- Publication date: September 2016
- URL: https://hbr.org/2016/09/know-your-customers-jobs-to-be-done
- Concept used: Understand the job/occasion that causes customers to choose a product, revealing substitutes that may cross conventional category lines.
- Layer 1 role: Demand-side framing for category evolution, substitution, and consumer/B2B use occasions when Layer 0 indicates ambiguity.
- Limitation: Qualitative Jobs-to-Be-Done narratives can become unfalsifiable; Layer 1 must connect them to observable buyer, usage, share, channel, or pricing evidence.

## 7. Ehrenberg-Bass Institute — Category Entry Points, Mental/Physical Availability, Category Growth
- Organization: Ehrenberg-Bass Institute for Marketing Science
- URL: https://marketingscience.info/learn-with-us/commercial-research
- Concept used: Category Entry Points are cues/situations linked to category buying; Mental and Physical Availability support brand competition; category-growth research emphasizes penetration and empirical norms.
- Layer 1 role: Conditional consumer-brand tool for penetration, occasions, availability, and buyer growth.
- Limitation: Not a universal framework for B2B, industrial, platform, or regulated monopolistic businesses; do not force consumer-brand metrics where they do not fit.

## 8. Michael J. Mauboussin & Dan Callahan — Market Share
- Publication date: September 15, 2022
- URL: https://www.morganstanley.com/im/en-us/individual-investor/insights/articles/market-share.html
- PDF: https://www.morganstanley.com/im/publication/insights/articles/article_marketshare.pdf
- Concept used: Connect market share with life cycle, concentration, markups, intangibles, and return on investment to study sustainable competitive advantage.
- Layer 1 role: Force share analysis to ask whether gains reflect durable market power/economic advantage rather than treating share as an end in itself.
- Limitation: Market share is an outcome that can be bought with discounting, capacity, or capital; share growth must be reconciled with economics downstream.

## 9. Mauboussin & Callahan — Measuring the Moat
- Publication date: October 15, 2024
- URL: https://www.morganstanley.com/im/publication/insights/articles/article_measuringthemoat.pdf
- Concept used: Competitive strategy analysis links sustainable value creation to returns above the opportunity cost of capital and the durability of those returns.
- Layer 1 role: Support evidence-based share-gain mechanisms and handoff to the Business Economic Engine / Reinvestment layers.
- Limitation: Layer 1 must not duplicate full ROIC/moat-return analysis owned downstream.

## 10. Mauboussin & Callahan — Competitive Advantage Period
- Publication date: April 14, 2026
- URL: https://www.morganstanley.com/im/en-us/institutional-investor/insights/consilient-observer/the-neglected-value-driver.html
- Concept used: Value creation depends on return spread, reinvestment, and how long advantage persists; competition, maturation, and life cycle cause decay.
- Layer 1 role: Translate category/competitive evidence into external duration evidence, while leaving valuation and final Duration synthesis downstream.
- Limitation: CAP is often applied in valuation; Layer 1 uses only the competitive-duration logic and must not infer market-implied valuation.

## 11. McKinsey — Net Incrementality / Cannibalization
- Publication: 2020
- URL: https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/consumer-packaged-goods/our-insights/will-innovation-finally-add-up-for-consumer-goods-companies
- Concept used: Incrementality is new volume from new buyers/additional occasions after netting cannibalization; innovations/channels can shift rather than create demand.
- Layer 1 role: Core rule for product/channel growth, including delivery/app/store shifts.
- Limitation: Precise incrementality often requires consumer- or transaction-level data unavailable to public investors; classify unresolved instead of fabricating a number.

## 12. McKinsey — Omnichannel Value and Cross-Channel Effects
- Publication: 2021 and related retail research
- URL: https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/growth-marketing-and-sales/our-insights/the-survival-guide-to-omnichannel-and-the-path-to-value
- URL: https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/retail/our-insights/adapting-to-the-next-normal-in-retail-the-customer-experience-imperative
- Concept used: Channels can create halo/recapture effects or cannibalize one another; omnichannel value must be assessed across the whole customer ecosystem.
- Layer 1 role: Prevent treating delivery/e-commerce/app/store growth as independent additive revenue vectors.
- Limitation: Retail examples do not automatically transfer to marketplaces, B2B sales, or industrial distribution; use channel-specific evidence.

## 13. Pankaj Ghemawat — CAGE Distance
- Publication date: September 2001
- URL: https://store.hbr.org/product/distance-still-matters-the-hard-reality-of-global-expansion/R0108K
- Concept used: Cultural, Administrative, Geographic, and Economic distance create costs/risks that simple foreign-market size analysis ignores.
- Layer 1 role: International replication gate before treating a foreign TAM as equivalent to the home market.
- Limitation: CAGE identifies distance, not company-specific ability to overcome it; actual adoption and economics remain necessary.

## 14. Pankaj Ghemawat — AAA Triangle
- Publication date: March 2007
- URL: https://hbr.org/2007/03/managing-differences-the-central-challenge-of-global-strategy
- Concept used: Adaptation, Aggregation, and Arbitrage are different responses to cross-border differences.
- Layer 1 role: Explain how a company is attempting to replicate internationally and what must remain standardized versus localized.
- Limitation: AAA is a strategic response taxonomy, not proof of successful replication.

## 15. Michael Mankins & Richard Steele — Turning Great Strategy into Great Performance
- Publication date: July–August 2005
- URL: https://hbr.org/2005/07/turning-great-strategy-into-great-performance
- Concept used: Strategy promises frequently fail to translate into realized performance; assumptions, resource deployment, and continuous tracking matter.
- Layer 1 role: Support `Promise → Action → Result` tracking for growth initiatives rather than grading management by narrative quality.
- Limitation: Organizational execution research cannot be fully observed from public filings; use only externally verifiable commitments/actions/results.
