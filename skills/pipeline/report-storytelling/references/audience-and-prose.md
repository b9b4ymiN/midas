# Audience and editorial style — full report, not a summary

## Defaults

- Audience `beginner-investor`. Assume familiarity with P/E, EPS, D/E, dividends, balance-sheet basics; do not introduce each as a new lesson.
- Do not assume WACC, beta relevering, lease-adjusted ROIC, ROIC-WACC comparability, FCFF/FCFE, perpetual securities, terminal value, reverse DCF, SOTP, multiple normalization, or return geometry.
- Never address an adult investor as a child. Avoid glib metaphors that misstate accounting or risk.
- Thai stock output is fluent Thai prose. Keep conventional English acronyms on first use, explaining purpose and correct Thai equivalent in context.
- Do not use `->`, `→`, `↑`, `↓` as replacements for complete explanations of change or causality in paragraphs. A labeled process/financial diagram can contain directional symbols if its relationships are actually supported.

## Writing contract

Every major section should have:
1. Headline stating an observed, qualified takeaway, not merely a topic label.
2. Intro paragraph saying what is happening, with timeframe.
3. Evidence with source (official/aggregator/model), units, bases and dates.
4. Explanation of *why* the evidence matters economically to the thesis or limitation.
5. When necessary, a fully attributed exhibit or worked example.
6. Risks, alternate reading and what remains unproven.

Allow the document to breathe: clear paragraphs, not a slash-delimited stream of ratios. Use one principal idea per paragraph. Full report ≠ short report: preserve valuation builds, assumption paths, financial tables, scenarios and risk triggers, moving deep math into traceable notes as necessary.

## Finance education ladder

For an advanced concept, use this order:
- **Purpose:** "What business question are we asking?"
- **Meaning:** plain-language distinction from related ideas.
- **Worked example:** simple, small numbers with fully stated assumptions. Label it illustrative, not company facts.
- **Real case:** source period, units and basis, and what changes when assumptions change.
- **Limit:** what the figure does **not** demonstrate.

Illustration: WACC is the blended opportunity cost to providers of business capital, not a company's total interest bill; ROIC measures an operating return on capital under a specific accounting definition. Subtract them **only when the lease/accounting/capital bases are comparable**. Never report a positive spread with mismatched definitions as definitive proof of a moat.

## Source fidelity

- Source reports can be wrong. It is acceptable to write "The source report states X, but its arithmetic appears inconsistent with Y"; unacceptable to silently replace the model.
- Preserve source author's confidence level: conjecture remains conjecture.
- Preserve competing scenarios; do not omit the Bear case because it complicates the story.
- Market price, analyst fair value and estimated forward earnings are categorically different observations.
- Allow section reordering **only if the source crosswalk remains complete**.

## Reader usability tests

A reader who only scans the headlines should understand the main argument without being pushed toward a recommendation stronger than the source. A reader who reads fully should be able to reconstruct major numerical claims and trace to the original analytic source. A novice should not have to decode unexplained jargon chains.
