# Thesis Patterns — Setup Archetypes

The investment plan differs by what *kind* of investment this is. Identify the archetype, then shape entry, sizing, horizon, and what-to-watch accordingly. Read alongside Step 3 of `SKILL.md`. Most real situations are a blend — name the dominant one.

Each archetype links back to the financial-health diagnostics from `company-valuation` (the ROIC−WACC spread, capex-vs-FCF, leverage trend) and the life-cycle stage from `business-narrative`.

---

## 1. Quality compounder at a fair price
**Signature:** wide, stable or widening ROIC−WACC spread; durable moat; mature-growth or mature-stable stage; reinvestment that is productive (capex up *with* FCF).
**The bet:** time and compounding. Value grows; you do not need a cheap entry, you need durability.
**Plan:** can be bought at or modestly below fair value; smaller required margin of safety (~10–15%). Size up with conviction. Long horizon. **Watch:** moat erosion, margin pressure, capital-allocation discipline, and any narrowing of the ROIC−WACC spread — the spread *is* the thesis.

## 2. Deep value / turnaround
**Signature:** trades below asset or normalised-earnings value; thin, zero, or negative spread; a problem the market is extrapolating; often mature or decline stage.
**The bet:** mean reversion / fix, contingent on a catalyst. Without a catalyst this is a value trap.
**Plan:** demand a large margin of safety (~35–50%+); smaller initial position; require an identifiable catalyst (new management, asset sale, restructuring, cycle turn) and a date or trigger. Medium horizon. **Watch:** the catalyst's progress and the balance sheet (turnarounds die of leverage). Define the exit if the fix stalls.

## 3. Cyclical at mid-cycle
**Signature:** earnings swing with a cycle (energy, semis, industrials, commodities, shipping); reported margins/multiples mislead at the extremes.
**The bet:** normalised mid-cycle earnings, bought when sentiment is at the trough.
**Plan:** value on through-cycle/normalised earnings, never peak or trough. Buy below mid-cycle value when sentiment is washed out; trim into euphoria. Beware the trap of a "cheap" trailing P/E at the peak (earnings about to fall) and an "expensive" one at the trough (earnings about to rise). Horizon = the cycle. **Watch:** the cycle drivers (capacity, inventory, spreads, demand) more than the quarter.

## 4. Growth at a reasonable price (GARP)
**Signature:** high-growth or mature-growth stage; growth is real but the price already embeds a lot of it; the question is whether reinvestment out-earns its cost.
**The bet:** growth that creates value (ROIC > WACC) compounding into a price that is not yet pricing all of it.
**Plan:** pay for growth only where reinvestment quality supports it; moderate margin of safety (~20–30%) because the downside if growth decelerates is real. Stage in; growth re-rates are volatile. Medium-to-long horizon. **Watch:** the growth rate's trajectory (deceleration is the classic GARP killer), the margin path, and whether incremental returns hold as it scales.

---

## How to use

1. Place the company on this map using the snapshot diagnostics and life-cycle stage.
2. Set the **margin of safety** from the archetype (cross-check with the `scenario_math.md` sizing guide and the upstream confidence level).
3. Set **sizing and horizon** from the archetype.
4. Pull the **conviction-builders and thesis-breakers** that are characteristic of the archetype (e.g., spread for a compounder; catalyst for a turnaround; cycle drivers for a cyclical; growth trajectory for GARP).

If the company is a genuine blend (e.g., a cyclical with a quality franchise), name both and let the more conservative posture govern the entry discount.
