# Investment Hook Patterns

Use this file to think, not to run a checklist. A hook is useful only if it changes the investor's risk/reward at the current price. If no hook is material, say so.

## Hook Test

A valid Key Investment Insight should answer:

1. Why is this stock interesting now?
2. What number or mechanism proves it?
3. How does that number compare with the current share price, market cap, enterprise value, yield, payout, or fair-value gap?
4. What must be true for the hook to matter?
5. What would make the hook invalid?

## Common Patterns

| Pattern | What to translate | Good output shape |
|---|---|---|
| Look-through asset value | Listed stake or investment value | Stake value per parent share vs current parent share price; or stake value as % of parent market cap |
| Net cash / investments | Cash and securities net of debt | Net cash per share and net cash as % of market cap |
| Segment worth more than the company | SOTP segment value | Segment value as % of market cap; implied residual value for the rest |
| High ROIC but low valuation | ROIC-WACC or ROE-COE spread vs P/B or EV/EBIT | "Market prices this like mediocre capital, but it earns high returns" |
| Trough earnings mispricing | Normalized profit vs depressed current profit | Normalized P/E or EV/EBIT vs trough multiple |
| Capital return | Dividend plus buyback | Shareholder yield and whether FCF covers it |
| Governance / capital allocation catalyst | Policy, listing rule, owner action, buyback, asset sale | What gap could close and what evidence would confirm it |
| Sentiment washout | Price reaction vs fundamentals | What bad news is priced in and what execution would reset expectations |

## Suzuki Case Example

Suzuki Motor (`7269.T`) showed why raw SOTP numbers are not enough.

Weak presentation:

> Maruti stake = about ¥4.01T.

Investor-ready hook:

> Suzuki owns roughly 58.5% of Maruti Suzuki India. That stake is worth about ¥4.01T, or about ¥2,080 per Suzuki share, compared with a Suzuki share price around ¥1,866. In plain English: the listed Maruti stake alone is worth more than the parent share price, before assigning value to Suzuki's remaining auto, motorcycle, marine, net cash, and other operations.

Why this matters:

- It turns a complicated parent/subsidiary SOTP into a simple price comparison.
- It shows the market is assigning little or negative residual value to the rest of Suzuki.
- It makes the investment question concrete: is the discount justified, or can India exposure plus governance/capital-efficiency catalysts close part of it?

Why it is not a rule:

- A listed stake is not automatically a hook.
- It matters only when the stake is large enough versus parent market value and when there is a plausible mechanism for the market to recognize it.
- If the stake is small, illiquid, structurally trapped, or already fully reflected in the parent price, keep it as a secondary detail.
