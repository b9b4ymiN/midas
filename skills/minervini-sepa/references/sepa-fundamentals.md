# Gate 1 — Q33 Fundamentals: Champion Stock Screen

The first gate of SEPA. I do not buy average companies hoping for average returns. I hunt **superperformers** — companies with explosive, accelerating growth and a real catalyst. Fundamental strength without a technical setup means **no buy**; but I will not even *look* at the chart of a company that fails here.

This gate runs **four categories** (A: Earnings, B: Sales, C: Story, D: Supply/Demand). Each item is tagged **MUST** (fail it and the gate fails), **PLUS** (adds conviction, not required), or **WATCH** (qualitative, flag concerns). The thresholds differ by market — see the two tables below.

---

## Category A — Earnings must jump

This is the heart of a superperformer. I want earnings exploding, not creeping.

| Item | Tag | Strict (US) | Adapted (SET / lower-growth markets) |
|---|---|---|---|
| EPS latest quarter YoY growth | **MUST** | ≥ 25% (prefer 50%+, love 100%+) | ≥ 15% (prefer 25%+) |
| EPS accelerating Q3 > Q2 > Q1 | **MUST** | sequential acceleration | acceleration or clearly sustained high growth |
| Annual EPS growth, 3-5 yr | **PLUS** | ≥ 25%/yr | ≥ 10%/yr |
| Next-year EPS estimate > this year | **MUST** | analyst consensus rising | same |
| No EPS losses in last 2 years | **MUST** | no annual loss | same; allow one COVID-type anomaly with clear recovery |

**Why EPS growth is non-negotiable:** cost-cutting can fake one or two quarters, but sustained EPS growth requires real revenue and margin discipline. Without it, I'm trading a story, not a business — and stories break.

**What I love:** earnings turning from flat or negative to sharply positive (the "earnings inflection"). One of the most powerful setups is a company whose EPS went from 0.10 → 0.20 → 0.40 — acceleration compounds.

---

## Category B — Sales must support

Earnings without revenue is a red flag — it usually means cost-cutting, buybacks, or one-offs that won't last. I want the top line confirming the bottom line.

| Item | Tag | Strict (US) | Adapted (SET / lower-growth markets) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue latest quarter YoY growth | **MUST** | ≥ 20-25% | ≥ 10% |
| Revenue accelerating last 2-3 Qs | **PLUS** | accelerating | sustained or re-accelerating |
| Gross margin stable or expanding | **MUST** | not compressing materially | same; watch input-cost cycles |

**Margin discipline:** if revenue is growing 30% but gross margin is collapsing from 40% to 30%, the "growth" is being bought with pricing or mix that won't hold. I pass.

---

## Category C — Story & catalyst (the New Factor)

The best superperformers almost always have a **New Factor** — something genuinely new driving the growth. This is qualitative but it's why I own the stock, not just rent the chart. Look for:

- **New product or service** reshaping an industry (e.g. a breakthrough chip, a platform shift).
- **New management** with a credible turnaround or expansion plan.
- **New expanding market** — a geographic or segment opening up.
- **New favorable industry conditions** — supply crunch, regulatory tailwind, capacity shortage.

If the only story is "earnings went up," be cautious. The strongest runs have a narrative *behind* the numbers that explains why the growth is durable, not a blip. Tag this **PLUS** but weight it heavily when present — a real New Factor is what separates a 50% mover from a 500% superperformer.

---

## Category D — Supply & demand structure

Who's buying, and how hard is it for them to move the stock?

| Item | Tag | What I look for |
|---|---|---|
| Float size | **PLUS** | Smaller float is better — demand moves it faster. Large mega-cap floats need huge volume to move. |
| Institutional ownership | **PLUS** | **Increasing** is the signal (smart money accumulating); already-very-high ownership leaves less room to buy. |
| Insider buying | **PLUS** | Insiders know more than we do. Meaningful open-market purchases are a green flag. |
| Buyback program | **PLUS** | Reduces supply, lifts EPS — supportive but not a thesis on its own. |

These don't fail the gate, but they sharpen conviction. A small-float, newly-installed institutional favorite with insider buying and a New Factor is exactly the soil superperformers grow in.

---

## Verdict for Gate 1

- **PASS** — all MUST items met (under the chosen lens), at least one strong PLUS, story makes sense.
- **CONDITIONAL** — MUST items met but thin (e.g. one strong quarter, no clear acceleration), or margins softening. Proceed to Gate 2 but flag the weakness; the technical setup had better be exceptional to compensate.
- **FAIL** — any MUST item missed under the chosen lens. I stop. No chart can rescue a non-superperformer.

## The two lenses — be explicit

I judge every ticker under a **declared lens** and say so in the output. Never switch mid-analysis.

- **Strict (US mega-cap / growth):** the original SEPA numbers. EPS Q YoY ≥ 25%, revenue Q YoY ≥ 20-25%, accelerating, RS ≥ 70. This is where champion stocks are most abundant.
- **Adapted (SET and other lower-growth / thinner markets):** Thai listed companies structurally grow slower than US growth stocks; applying 25% EPS gates would reject the whole market. I lower EPS Q to ≥ 15% and revenue Q to ≥ 10%, but I keep the **discipline the same**: MUST items still fail the gate, Trend Template still runs hard, risk rules still bind. The lens adjusts *levels*, never *rigor*.

When in doubt, I use the **stricter** threshold and say why. A stock that only clears Adapted but not Strict gets labelled as such — the user knows the conviction bar is lower.
