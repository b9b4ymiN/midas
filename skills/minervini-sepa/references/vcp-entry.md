# Gate 3 — VCP: Volatility Contraction Pattern

The third gate of SEPA and my signature setup — **the most reliable pattern I have ever seen.** VCP shows me when a stock has been properly digested and is ready to move, with volume telling me whether institutions are accumulating or distributing. This is where I find my entry.

A stock that passed Gates 1 (fundamentals) and 2 (Stage 2 trend) is *qualified to buy* — but not yet *at the right time*. VCP is the timing mechanism.

---

## What VCP is

After a Stage 2 advance, a healthy stock consolidates — it digests the prior gain, shakes out weak hands, and builds energy for the next leg up. **Volatility Contraction Pattern** describes *how* a proper consolidation behaves: each successive pullback within the base is **smaller in both percentage and duration** than the one before, while volume **dries up** into the lows. Supply is being exhausted. Then a breakout on **surging volume** confirms institutions are stepping in.

The opposite — a base where pullbacks get *larger* and volume *expands* on the dips — is distribution, not consolidation. I avoid that.

---

## Pattern anatomy

A VCP builds through successive **contractions** (C1, C2, C3...) and resolves at the **pivot**.

| Phase | Typical size | Behavior |
|---|---|---|
| **C1** | ~25% pullback from base high | First, largest shakeout. Often heavy volume at the low, then stabilizes. |
| **C2** | ~15% pullback | Tighter. Sellers less aggressive. |
| **C3** | ~8% pullback | Tightest. Volume drying up. |
| **Tight action** | 5-10 days, almost no movement | Price goes flat, swings tiny, volume completely dry. Supply exhausted — breakout imminent. |
| **Pivot** | highest price of the final contraction | The line a break above completes the pattern. |
| **Breakout** | pivot + | Volume surges ≥ 40-50% above average. Institutional confirmation. |

The contraction sizes (25/15/8) are typical examples, not rigid. The **principle** is the direction: each contraction smaller than the last. A 30 → 18 → 10 → 4 sequence is a gorgeous VCP. A 20 → 22 → 25 sequence is a broken base.

---

## Four characteristics of a complete VCP

All four must be present:

1. **Contractions decrease each time** (C1 > C2 > C3) in **both** percentage *and* duration. The sellers are losing conviction with each shake.
2. **Volume dries up during pullbacks.** Down days into the contraction lows should be on fading volume — no forced selling, no institutional dumping. Supply is exhausted.
3. **At least 2 contractions** (T2 minimum). I **prefer 3** (T3). More contractions = more shaking out = more reliable.
4. **Breakout volume surges ≥ 40-50% above average.** This confirms real institutional buying, not retail noise. A breakout on flat or weak volume is suspect — I wait for volume or I don't buy.

Miss any of these and it's not a clean VCP. I either wait for it to tighten further or I pass.

---

## VCP types (T-rating)

| Type | Contractions | Reliability | My response |
|---|---|---|---|
| **T2** | 2 | Moderate | Acceptable **only if fundamentals are very strong**. Smaller size, tighter stop. |
| **T3** | 3 | High | **My preferred setup.** Standard size. High confidence. |
| **T4** | 4 | Very high | Rare. When I find one with great fundamentals, I **size up**. |

The T-rating is conviction, not just pattern count — a T3 with textbook tight action and surging breakout volume is one of my highest-conviction entries.

---

## Tight action — the tell

In the final contraction before the pivot, I look for **"tight action"**: the price stops moving. Daily ranges collapse, the stock closes nearly unchanged for 5-10 sessions, and volume **completely dries up**. This is the market telling me supply is exhausted — there's no one left to sell. A breakout from tight action is far more reliable than one from loose, choppy action.

Loose action — big daily ranges, erratic closes, heavy volume on down days within the base — is the opposite signal. Even if the trend and fundamentals pass, loose action means the stock isn't ready. I wait for it to tighten or I move on.

---

## The pivot and entry zone

- **Pivot price** = the highest price of the final contraction (the resistance line the breakout must exceed). Once price breaks above this, the pattern is complete.
- **Entry zone** = pivot price **+ 0 to 5%**. I buy the breakout, not the anticipation and not the chase.
- **Do not chase.** If price has already run well past the entry zone (e.g. +10-15% on the breakout day), the R-multiple has degraded and the risk has expanded. I let it go or wait for a pullback to the pivot — I do not chase extended entries.

---

## Breakout volume — the confirmation

The breakout day must show **volume ≥ 40-50% above the stock's average** (use the 50-day average volume as the baseline). This is my confirmation that institutions — not retail — are doing the buying. Without it, the breakout is unconfirmed and I treat it as a no-trade:

- Breakout on ≥ 1.5× average volume → strong confirmation. Proceed.
- Breakout on 1.0-1.4× → marginal. Reduce size or wait for a follow-through day.
- Breakout on below-average volume → **no confirmation.** Do not buy. Often a fake-out.

---

## When there is no VCP — classify honestly

Not every Stage 2 stock has a VCP right now. Be honest about which of these is true:

- **Clean VCP present** → Gate 3 PASS. Proceed to risk geometry.
- **Base forming but not yet tight** → "forming." State what's needed (e.g. "one more contraction to under 10%, volume to dry"). I wait.
- **Stage 2 but extended** (already ran far past a pivot, no pullback to speak of) → "extended." I wait for a pullback to the 20/50-day MA or a new base. I do not chase.
- **Choppy / loose / distribution in the base** → "no clean setup." Even with great fundamentals and trend, I pass on the entry until the action tightens.

---

## Verdict for Gate 3

- **PASS** — clean VCP (T2/T3/T4), decreasing contractions, dry volume on dips, tight action, clear pivot, breakout volume confirmed (or the trigger defined). Proceed to Gate 4.
- **TRIGGER DEFINED** — pattern nearly there; state the exact trigger ("a break of ฿X on ≥ 1.5× volume"). I wait for it.
- **NO SETUP** — base not formed, extended, or loose. I sit on my hands. Naming the next observable event that would change this.

I do not manufacture a VCP. If it isn't there, I say so — patience is a massive edge, and forcing a trade that isn't there is how you lose.
