# Gate 4 — Risk Rules: Stops, Sizing, Selling, Discipline

The fourth and final gate of SEPA. This is the gate that decides whether I *actually make money* over time. Picking winners is easy — anyone can do it occasionally. Managing losers and sizing positions by risk, not by feelings, is what separates champions from the crowd.

"The most important thing I learned is to size my positions based on **risk**, not on how confident I feel." Confidence is an emotion; risk is math. I do the math.

---

## The hard stop — my #1 rule

**Always cut losses at 7-8%.** This is the one rule I never bend, regardless of the setup, the story, or how much I "believe" in the stock.

- **Standard stop** = the **low of the final contraction** (the VCP's last shakeout low). If price breaches that, the pattern is broken — exit, no debate.
- **Maximum risk per trade** = 5-8% of entry price. If the natural stop would be further than 8% away, the entry is wrong (too early, too extended) — I either wait for a tighter entry or I pass.
- **Great setup with tight action** can use a **5%** stop. Tighter stops mean more shares at the same dollar risk — that's the reward for a high-quality VCP.

### Why 7-8% is sacred — the break-even math

The deeper the loss, the larger the gain needed to recover. This is why I cut early:

| Loss | Gain needed to break even |
|---|---|
| -8% | +8.7% (acceptable) |
| -15% | +17.6% (borderline) |
| -25% | +33.3% (dangerous) |
| -50% | +100% (catastrophic) |

A -50% position needs a double just to get back to flat. I refuse to put myself in that hole. The 7-8% rule keeps every loss recoverable with a single normal up-move.

### Strict prohibitions

- **Never average down.** Adding to a loser is how small losses become catastrophic ones. If my thesis was wrong, I exit — I don't double down on hope.
- **Never pyramid downward.** Same principle. I only add to *winners* (see below), never to losers.
- **Never hold on hope.** If the stop hits, I'm out. "The stock doesn't know you own it" — only the price is real.

## Position sizing — risk first, not confidence

I size every position by **how much I'm willing to lose**, not by how much I expect to make.

### The formula (2% risk per trade)

$$\text{Shares} = \frac{\text{Portfolio} \times \text{Risk\%}}{\text{Entry Price} - \text{Stop Price}}$$

- **Risk per trade:** capped at **1-2% of total portfolio**. I use 1% for lesser-conviction or Adapted-lens setups, 2% for textbook T3/T4 with breakout confirmation.
- **Entry − Stop** = the per-share risk (the dollar distance to the stop).

**Worked example:**
- Portfolio = ฿1,000,000. Risk 1% = ฿10,000.
- Entry = ฿100, Stop = ฿92 → per-share risk = ฿8.
- Shares = ฿10,000 / ฿8 = **1,250 shares** (฿125,000 position size).

Notice: I don't decide "I'll invest ฿125,000." I decide "I'll risk ฿10,000," and the math tells me the position size. That's the discipline — risk defines size, always.

## Reward-to-risk — the 3:1 minimum

Every trade must offer **at least 3:1 reward-to-risk** before I enter. This is non-negotiable because my win rate is realistically 40-50% (I'm wrong often — I just don't *stay* wrong). At 1:3 R:R:

- Win 45% of trades at +3R, lose 55% at -1R → **net +0.8R per trade**, strongly profitable over time.
- Drop to 1:1 R:R and that same win rate **loses money**.

**R-multiple** = (Target − Entry) / (Entry − Stop). I state it explicitly for every setup. If it's under 3:1, I either find a better target, a tighter stop, or I pass.

---

## The pyramid — adding to winners

I pyramid only into strength — never into weakness. Each add is smaller than the last, and only after the prior tranche is in profit and the trend is confirming.

| Tranche | When | Size of total position | Logic |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Initial entry** | Breakout from pivot | **50-60%** of intended position | Core position, entered when risk is lowest (stop is tight, just under the pivot). |
| **Add 1** | +10-15% from entry | **30%** | Confirms the trade is working. Add as price proves itself. |
| **Add 2** | +20-25% from entry | **10-20%** | Smaller size — risk is higher further from the pivot. |

After each add, I trail the stop up so that the *total* dollar risk never exceeds the original 1-2%. Adding does not mean increasing risk — it means the position has earned its size by performing.

## Sell rules — taking profits and trailing

I don't use fixed price targets. I let winners run via trailing stops and let the price action tell me when to exit. The rules, in order of importance:

1. **Stop hit → exit instantly.** No deliberation. This overrides everything.
2. **At +15% gain → move stop to breakeven.** The trade is now risk-free; I let it run for free.
3. **At +20-25% gain quickly (within 1-3 weeks) → consider selling 1/3** to lock in a chunk, unless momentum is explosive (see Eight-Week Rule).
4. **At +25% gain → move stop to +10%.** Locking in a real profit while giving room.
5. **Breach of the 50-day MA on heavy volume → sell all.** This is a distribution signal — institutions are selling. I don't wait for the stop if the 50-day fails on big volume.
6. **Story breaks → sell.** An earnings miss, a slashed guidance, a failed catalyst — the thesis is invalidated. I'm out regardless of the chart.

### The Eight-Week Rule — when NOT to sell

If a stock gains **20%+ in the first 3 weeks** after my entry, it's behaving like a **superperformer**. The best winners give *no* early sell signals — they just keep going. In that case I **hold for the full 8 weeks** before considering a sell, because selling a future 300% winner for a 25% gain is the most expensive mistake in this game. Power wins get room.

## Market condition — set the exposure dial

My individual trade discipline is meaningless if I'm fully invested in a bear market. I read the **market condition** (the index, not the stock) and set my total exposure accordingly. This is the meta-rule above all the trade rules.

| Market condition | What it looks like | My exposure | How I trade |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Confirmed Uptrend** | Index in Stage 2, follow-through days, breadth expanding | **75-100%** | Aggressive. Find setups, build positions, pyramid winners. |
| **Uptrend Under Pressure** | Index still up but distribution days rising, leaders acting loose | **40-60%** | Cautious. Smaller sizes, raise stops, no new aggressive adds. |
| **Rally Attempt** | Index trying to recover from a correction; no confirmed follow-through | **10-30%** | Wait and watch. Very few trades, smallest size, tightest stops. |
| **Distribution / Downtrend** | Index in Stage 4, heavy selling, breakdowns | **0-10%** | **Hold cash, protect capital.** No new buys. The best trade is often no trade. |

**Cash is a position.** Being in cash during a downtrend is not "missing out" — it's *protecting the capital I need to deploy when the next uptrend begins.* Most traders can't do this; they feel compelled to be invested. That compulsion is why most underperform.

## The psychology — process over outcome

"The market is a mirror. It reflects your psychology back to you." Fear and greed are the enemies; the system is the defense.

- **Process over outcome.** If I followed the system perfectly and the trade lost, that's a good trade. If I broke the rules and won, that's a bad trade — because it teaches me to break the rules again.
- **Think in probabilities.** With a 40-50% win rate and 3:1 R:R, long-term profit is a mathematical certainty. Any single trade is noise.
- **Kill the ego.** "I'm often wrong — but I never stay wrong." The stop makes this literal.
- **Don't trade when unready** (tired, stressed, angry, revenge-seeking after a loss). Rest, review, return.

### Six habits I keep

1. Review the watchlist every evening — plan ahead, never decide mid-market.
2. Keep a trade journal — log the reason for every entry and exit, and every mistake.
3. Study historical superperformers — build pattern recognition in the mind.
4. Check market condition daily — distribution vs follow-through.
5. Don't trade when unready.
6. **Celebrate discipline, not profit.** Following the process perfectly is the true win; the money follows.

## Verdict for Gate 4

- **PASS** — entry zone defined, stop within 7-8%, target gives ≥ 3:1, position size set at 1-2% risk, pyramid and sell rules planned, market condition supportive. I have a complete trade. Proceed to the final verdict.
- **CONDITIONAL** — geometry is acceptable but market condition is "Under Pressure" → reduce size, raise stop, smaller initial tranche.
- **FAIL** — R-multiple under 3:1, stop too far, or market in Distribution. No trade. I wait for better geometry or better market conditions. **Protecting capital always beats forcing a trade.**
